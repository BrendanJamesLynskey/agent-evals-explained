<Layer kind="maths">

Let $\mu$ be the chance one attempt at a task is right and $s = \sqrt{\rho}$. Each attempt copies the run's shared outcome $Z \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\mu)$ with probability $s$ and otherwise draws its own, so two attempts correlate at exactly $\rho$ (chapter 1's construction). Given $Z = 1$ an attempt is right with probability $s + (1 - s)\mu$; given $Z = 0$, with probability $(1 - s)\mu$; attempts are independent given $Z$. Summing over $Z$ gives the equation above for $k$ attempts. With $\rho = 0$ it is $1 - (1 - \mu)^k$, which reaches 1; with $\rho > 0$ the runs where the model has misread the task ($Z = 0$) improve only at rate $(1 - s)\mu$, so more turns help less and less. That is the flattening in the bars as the budget goes from 4 to 8 to 16.

With a shell, a budget of $B$ turns allows about $(B - 2)/2$ attempts: one turn to read, then an edit and a test per attempt, and one to answer. Without a shell, $k = 1$ whatever the budget.
